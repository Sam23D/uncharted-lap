# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-02)

First public release. Web first; Linux and Windows builds are also provided.

### The game
- **Trade:** 14 goods in four categories (food, spices, textiles, metals) across 12 ports. Each port sells a few goods cheaply and buys everything.
- **Charts and cartographers:** start with 4 home ports, with the rest of the chart under fog. Hire cartographers in Lisbon, London, Madeira and Elmina to chart the Mediterranean, the Baltic, the Guinea coast and the Cape route.
- **Fleet:** Sloop, Cutter, Fluyt, Brig, Carrack and Galleon. Buying a Galleon (1200 gold, sold in Genoa and Calicut) wins. There is no day limit.
- **Crew:** deckhands, navigators, cooks, marksmen and swordsmen with daily wages; unpaid crew desert.
- **Sea voyages:** food and water rations, a day/night cycle at sea, and pirates on about 1 in 5 trips. Each region has its own pirates; run or fight with cannons, muskets and boarders.
- **Ship's log:** every trip, encounter and trade with its margin, grouped by day.
- **Title screen** with ships sailing by, **Continue**, and **Credits**.

### New in this release
- **Autosave:** a single slot, saved in every port, with Continue on the title screen. On the Web it is stored in the browser.
- **Esc menu:** Resume, Ship's log, Back to title, and Quit (desktop only). The end panel has a Title button.
- **First-voyage hint** in the Store, which you can dismiss.
- **Port emblems:** crests replace the thin flag pennants.
- **Text:** the softer dusk tint at sea; text uses only characters the Web font can draw.
- **Builds:** 1280×720 window with crisp integer scaling, an app icon, the version on the title screen, and the Compatibility renderer for the Web.
- **Linux:** accessibility (AccessKit) is turned off, which avoided a crash on Linux sessions without an accessibility bus.
